# ENTREPRENEURSHIP EXPERTISE KNOWLEDGE BASE

## FOUNDER MINDSET

### Entrepreneurial Traits
Vision: See opportunities others miss
Resilience: Persist through failure
Adaptability: Change strategy when needed
Courage: Take calculated risks
Passion: Deep belief in mission
Resourcefulness: Do more with less
Curiosity: Always learning and questioning
Hustle: Work harder than employees
Ownership: Full accountability
Long-term thinking: Vision beyond next quarter

### Founder Journey Phases

Phase 1: Idea & Validation (Months 0-3)
- Define problem deeply
- Research market
- Test with users
- Validate assumptions
- Build first prototype

Phase 2: MVP & Traction (Months 3-12)
- Launch minimal product
- Get first 100 users
- Understand user needs
- Iterate rapidly
- Find product-market fit signals

Phase 3: Growth & Scaling (Year 1-2)
- Hire first team
- Establish processes
- Increase marketing spend
- Expand user base
- Seek funding if needed

Phase 4: Scale & Market Dominance (Year 2+)
- Build strong team
- Establish market leadership
- Expand to new markets/products
- Build moat (defensibility)
- Profitability or exit path

## IDEA VALIDATION

### Problem-Solution Fit
1. Define the problem deeply
   - Who has this problem?
   - How urgent is it?
   - How much would they pay to solve it?
   - Are there existing solutions?

2. Test your solution
   - Talk to 20+ potential users
   - Show prototypes
   - Get honest feedback
   - Iterate based on feedback

3. Signals of fit
   - Users asking for it
   - Willingness to pay
   - Repeat usage
   - Word of mouth
   - User testimonials

### Market Research
- Market size: TAM (Total Addressable Market)
- Growth rate: Is market growing?
- Competition: Who else is solving this?
- Trends: Is this trending up?
- Regulations: Legal landscape?

TAM Calculation Example:
- 1 billion people globally
- 10% in your geographic region = 100 million
- 20% can afford your solution = 20 million
- Your TAM = 20 million users or dollars

## BUSINESS MODELS

### SaaS (Software as a Service)
Model: Monthly/annual subscription

Pros:
- Predictable recurring revenue
- Scalable (one product, many users)
- High margins
- Easy to measure
- Customer lifetime value models well

Cons:
- Competitive market
- Customer acquisition expensive
- Churn is critical metric
- Needs product excellence

Examples: Salesforce, Slack, HubSpot, Notion

### Marketplace
Model: Commission on transactions between buyers/sellers

Pros:
- Network effects (more sellers = more buyers)
- Both sides invested
- High switching cost
- Revenue scales with transaction volume

Cons:
- Chicken-egg problem (need both sides)
- Moderation challenges
- Fraud/trust issues
- Takes longer to scale

Examples: Uber, Airbnb, Fundi Connect model

### Freemium
Model: Free basic version, paid premium features

Pros:
- Low friction to user acquisition
- Viral potential
- Easy to convert best customers
- Data collection

Cons:
- Most users stay free (low conversion)
- High infrastructure costs
- Hard to convert free to paid
- May cannibalize premium

Examples: Dropbox, Spotify, Slack

### Advertising
Model: Free product, revenue from ads

Pros:
- Massive user base possible
- Network effects
- Viral growth potential
- High revenue potential at scale

Cons:
- User experience conflict
- Need massive scale
- Hard to build trust
- Regulation risks

Examples: Facebook, Google, YouTube

### Direct Sales / B2B
Model: Sell directly to businesses

Pros:
- High contract value
- Relationship-based (defensible)
- Enterprise features justify premium
- Predictable revenue

Cons:
- Long sales cycles (3-12 months)
- High CAC (customer acquisition cost)
- Needs experienced sales team
- Slow growth initially

Examples: Salesforce, Stripe, Plaid

## FUNDING JOURNEY

### Pre-seed (Before idea validation)
- Founder savings
- Friends and family
- Bootstrapping
- Angel investors
- Grants
- Amount: $10K-100K
- Goal: Prove concept

### Seed (Product-market fit signals)
- Angel investors
- Seed funds
- Early VC
- Friends and family
- Amount: $500K-2M
- Goal: Build team, gain traction

### Series A (Clear traction)
- Venture capital firms
- Strategic investors
- Returning angels
- Amount: $2M-10M
- Goal: Scale rapidly, expand team, new markets

### Series B+ (Scale phase)
- Growth-focused VCs
- Later-stage funds
- Corporate investors
- Amount: $10M+
- Goal: Become category leader, international

## KEY METRICS (STARTUPS)

### User Metrics
- DAU/MAU: Daily/Monthly Active Users
- Signup rate: % converting to signup
- Viral coefficient: Users brought by each user
- Cohort retention: % returning after X days

### Revenue Metrics
- ARR/MRR: Annual/Monthly Recurring Revenue
- ARPU: Average Revenue Per User
- CAC: Customer Acquisition Cost
- LTV: Lifetime Value (total revenue per customer)
- LTV:CAC ratio: Should be 3:1 or better

### Efficiency Metrics
- Burn rate: Monthly spending
- Runway: Months of operation left
- Unit economics: Revenue vs cost per unit
- Payback period: How long to recover CAC

### Engagement Metrics
- Session length: Time spent
- Frequency: How often users return
- Feature adoption: % using key features
- NPS: Net Promoter Score (would recommend?)

## COMMON STARTUP MISTAKES

1. Building without validating: Building what you think users want
2. Solving personal problem only: Market too small
3. Too many features: MVP bloat, slow to launch
4. No clear differentiation: Why would users choose you?
5. Ignoring unit economics: Growth that loses money
6. Hiring too fast: Overhead before revenue
7. Not tracking metrics: Flying blind
8. Poor communication: Team misaligned
9. Pivoting too often: No commitment
10. Burning out: Unsustainable pace early

## FUNDRAISING STRATEGY

### Before Raising
- Clear story: Why now? Why you? Why this market?
- Strong metrics: Show traction
- Use of funds: What will money do?
- Team: Who will execute?
- Defensibility: Why can't competitors copy?

### Pitch Deck (10 slides)
1. Problem: What problem you're solving
2. Solution: How you solve it
3. Market: How big is the opportunity?
4. Product: What do you build?
5. Traction: What progress to date?
6. Team: Who is executing?
7. Business model: How do you make money?
8. Financials: Projections
9. Competition: Why you win?
10. Ask: How much, for what?

### Investor Types
- Angels: Individuals investing their own money
- Seed funds: Small funds for early stage
- VC firms: Large pools, professional investors
- Corporate VC: Companies investing in startups
- Impact investors: Returns + social impact

## BUILDING THE TEAM

### Early Hires (First 10 people)
Look for:
- Track record of execution
- Domain expertise
- Cultural fit
- Willingness to wear many hats
- Complementary skills to founder

### Co-founder Dynamics
- Complementary skills (technical + business)
- Shared vision
- Different perspectives
- Trust and communication
- Clear role definitions
- Equity split discussions upfront

### Company Culture
- Mission-driven: Why you exist
- Transparency: Open communication
- Autonomy: Trust your team
- Accountability: Clear ownership
- Continuous learning: Growth mindset
- Sustainable pace: Avoid burnout

## EXIT STRATEGIES

### Acquisition
- Company buys you outright
- Timeline: 3-7 years typical
- Returns: Depends on price
- Outcome: Founders may stay or leave
- Example: Instagram acquired by Facebook ($1B)

### IPO (Initial Public Offering)
- Go public, sell stock on exchanges
- Timeline: 7-10+ years
- Returns: Potentially unlimited
- Outcome: Company becomes public company
- Example: Facebook IPO ($38B valuation)

### Sustainable/Profitable Business
- Run forever
- Generate profits
- Pay dividends
- Bootstrap future growth
- Founder remains CEO indefinitely
- Example: Basecamp (early Stripe inspiration)

### Down/Acquihire
- Sold for team, not valuation
- Assets/patents acquired
- Smaller exit
- Team continues elsewhere
- Sometimes necessary outcome

- Vision clarity sustains you
- Mission matters

### Long-term Success
- Build defensible business
- Strong team is moat
- Customer satisfaction paramount
- Adapt to market changes
- Stay humble, learn always
