# NEGOTIATION TACTICS EXPERTISE KNOWLEDGE BASE

## NEGOTIATION FUNDAMENTALS

### What is Negotiation?
Definition: Discussion to reach mutually acceptable agreement

Not: Winning vs losing, tricking someone, manipulation
Yes: Problem-solving, creating value, long-term relationships

Negotiation mindset:
- Both sides better off than starting
- Information is currency
- Relationship matters
- Think long-term
- Creative solutions exist

---

## PREPARATION (Most Important!)

### Before Negotiation

Research thoroughly:
- Other party's interests
- Their alternatives (BATNA)
- Market standards
- Their constraints
- Your alternatives (BATNA)

Define positions:
- Your ideal outcome
- Your walkaway point (minimum acceptable)
- Opening position
- Concession strategy
- Walk-away strategy

Get information:
- What do they value?
- What are they concerned about?
- Who else is involved?
- What's their timeline?
- What's their budget?

Set goals:
- What specifically do you want?
- What would be great (ambitious)?
- What's acceptable (realistic)?
- What's unacceptable (walkaway)?

Example negotiation planning:
- Deal: Consulting agreement
- Ideal: $200/hr, 20 hrs/week, 6 months
- Great: $150/hr, flexible hours
- Acceptable: $120/hr, 10 hrs/week guaranteed
- Walkaway: Less than $100/hr or less than 5 hrs/week

## NEGOTIATION PHASES

### Phase 1: Opening
Set tone and establish anchors

Strategies:
- Make first offer (establishes anchor)
- Ask for more than you expect
- Know their starting point
- Listen to their opening

First offer importance:
- Significantly influences outcome
- But can backfire if unreasonable
- Anchoring effect is real
- Establish range, not exact

Tone setting:
- Be friendly, not aggressive
- Show respect
- Express genuine interest
- Set collaborative tone

---

### Phase 2: Information Exchange
Both sides present case

Share information strategically:
- Why your position makes sense
- What you're trying to accomplish
- Constraints you face
- Timeline pressure
- Budget limitations

Ask questions:
- Why is this important to you?
- What would ideal look like for you?
- What are your constraints?
- Who else is involved?
- What's your timeline?

Listen actively:
- Understand their real interests (not just positions)
- Find common ground
- Identify creative solutions
- Notice what's not being said

---

### Phase 3: Bargaining
Back-and-forth exchange

Making concessions:
- Never first to offer major concession
- Make small concessions early
- Link concessions (if you give, I get)
- Concession gets them to reciprocate
- Save big concessions for end

Requesting concessions:
- For every concession, ask for one
- Be specific (not vague)
- Explain why it matters
- Show impact on you
- Make it feel like a big ask

Trading concessions:
- "If we move to $150/hr, can you commit to 15 hours minimum?"
- "If we extend contract 12 months, can we reduce rate to $130/hr?"
- Both sides get something

Avoiding mistakes:
- Don't give without getting
- Don't accept first offer (signals more room)
- Don't let them see your bottom line
- Don't accept everything at once

---

### Phase 4: Problem-Solving
Creative solutions to impasse

Techniques:
- Expand pie (find new options)
- Find what they value most
- Find what you value most
- Trade on different values
- Create options, evaluate later

Example:
Deadlock on price: $200 vs $100
Solutions:
- Hybrid: $150 base + performance bonus
- Volume discount: $120/hr if 30 hrs/week
- Equity: Lower rate + equity stake
- Milestone-based: Different rates for phases
- Contingent: Lower price if milestones hit

---

### Phase 5: Closing
Move toward agreement

Confirming agreement:
- Summarize what's agreed
- Write down key terms
- Get confirmation from other side
- Document everything

Last-minute requests:
- Expected near end
- "Just one more thing..."
- Prepare for this
- Don't give away everything at end
- Keep something to trade

Finalizing:
- Put in writing quickly
- Both parties review
- No surprises
- Professional relationship maintained

## NEGOTIATION STYLES

### Competitive Negotiators
Goal: Win, other side loses

Approach:
- Aggressive
- Demanding
- Positional
- Demands concessions without giving

Outcome: Winner and loser, resentment

Use when: Truly zero-sum, one-time transaction

---

### Collaborative Negotiators
Goal: Both sides win, solve problem together

Approach:
- Sharing information
- Problem-solving mindset
- Creative solutions
- Build relationship

Outcome: Both satisfied, good relationship

Use when: Long-term relationship, complex deal, multiple issues

---

### Accommodating Negotiators
Goal: Maintain relationship, agree quickly

Approach:
- Accepting other's terms
- Minimizing conflict
- Giving concessions easily
- Avoiding negotiation

Outcome: Relationship intact, but may leave value on table

Use when: Relationship critical, small stakes, time matters

---

### Avoiding Negotiators
Goal: Don't negotiate, avoid conflict

Approach:
- Delay tactics
- Non-committal responses
- Postpone decision
- Evade direct negotiation

Outcome: No resolution, frustration

Use when: Not yet ready, need more information, truly inappropriate time

---

### Best Approach: Situational
- Mostly collaborative
- Get competitive when needed
- Accommodate when relationship crucial
- Understand when avoiding helpful

## TACTICS & STRATEGIES

### Power Tactics

BATNA (Best Alternative to Negotiated Agreement):
- Your backup plan if negotiation fails
- Strong BATNA = more power
- Weak BATNA = less power
- Communication of BATNA (implicit, not explicit)

Timeline pressure:
- Your deadline makes you weak
- Their deadline makes them weak
- Control timeline when possible
- Be aware of artificial deadlines

Walking away:
- Willingness to walk away = power
- Must be credible
- Know your walkaway point
- Walking away sometimes best option

---

### Psychological Tactics

Anchoring:
- First number sets range
- High anchor shifts negotiation up
- Low anchor shifts negotiation down
- Unrealistic anchor backfires

Reciprocity:
- If you concede, they feel obligated
- Concessions get reciprocal concessions
- One-sided conceding signals weakness

Framing:
- Same deal framed differently changes perception
- "$20k investment" vs "$20k return"
- Loss-framing creates urgency
- Gain-framing creates comfort

Scarcity:
- Artificial scarcity (can backfire)
- Real constraints create urgency
- Multiple options for them creates urgency

---

### Information Tactics

Strategic disclosure:
- Share information that helps your case
- Withhold information that hurts your case
- But lies destroy credibility long-term
- Be truthful but selective

Asking questions:
- Questions gather information
- Questions guide thinking
- Questions signal confidence
- Questions keep conversation going

Silence:
- Don't fill silences after offer
- Let them sit with your offer
- Signals confidence
- Makes them elaborate on position

## DIFFICULT SITUATIONS

### When They Won't Budge
- Understand their constraints
- Offer creative alternatives
- Expand the pie
- Take break, come back fresh
- Walk away if necessary

### When You're Losing Patience
- Take a break
- Don't let emotion drive decisions
- Stick to walkaway point
- Remember long-term relationship
- Breathe, don't react in anger

### When They Play Dirty
Tactics to avoid:
- Lying (destroys trust forever)
- Threatening (escalates conflict)
- Manipulation (creates resentment)
- Low-ball offers (signals disrespect)
- Yelling (shuts down communication)

Response:
- Call it out professionally
- Propose back to ethical negotiation
- Walk away if continues
- Document everything
- Don't match their tactics

### Multiple Parties
- Understand alliances
- Negotiate with decision-maker
- Coalition building
- One-on-one conversations before group
- Find common ground across parties

## DIFFERENT CONTEXTS

### Salary Negotiation
Preparation:
- Know market rate (Glassdoor, salary guides)
- Know your value (accomplishments, skills)
- Know their budget

Strategy:
- Let them make offer first (if possible)
- Don't disclose current salary
- Focus on value, not need
- Consider total package (benefits, equity, flexibility)
- Counter offer with reasoning

### Vendor/Supplier Negotiation
Leverage:
- Volume commitments
- Long-term contracts
- Other options
- Industry timing

Goals:
- Best price
- Good service
- Flexible terms
- Relationship for future needs

### Customer Negotiation
Leverage:
- Your solution uniqueness
- Their pain urgency
- Implementation timeline
- Value delivered

Goals:
- Fair price
- Happy customer
- Avoid margin compression
- Set for good relationship

---

## POST-NEGOTIATION

### After Agreement

Follow through:
- Do exactly what you agreed
- Be responsive
- Deliver on commitments
- Build trust for next negotiation

Documentation:
- Send written summary
- Get written confirmation
- Keep records
- Reference in future dealings

Relationship building:
- Regular check-ins
- Be reliable
- Be fair in dealings
- Build credibility for next negotiation

---

## COMMON NEGOTIATION MISTAKES

1. No preparation: Going in blind
2. Accepting first offer: Signals room exists
3. Revealing walkaway: Loses leverage
4. Emotions driving decisions: Bad long-term outcomes
5. All-or-nothing: Misses creative solutions
6. Ignoring relationship: Short-term wins, long-term losses
7. Lying: Destroys trust permanently
8. No BATNA: Weak negotiating position
9. Talking too much: Gives away information
10. Rushing: Leaves value on table
