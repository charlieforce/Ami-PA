# GOVERNMENT CONTRACT EXPERTISE KNOWLEDGE BASE

## FUNDAMENTALS

### What is Government Contracting?

Definition: Business contracts with federal, state/provincial, and local government agencies

Opportunity:
- Billions in government spending annually
- Stable, predictable payment
- Large contract values
- Ongoing relationships
- Prestige and credibility

Challenge:
- Complex regulations
- Extensive documentation
- Compliance requirements
- Slow payment timelines
- Competitive bidding

---

## USA GOVERNMENT CONTRACTING

### Overview

Federal spending:
- $600+ billion annually in contracts
- Defense Department largest buyer
- GSA, EPA, HHS, DOT, HUD also major buyers
- All levels: Federal, state, local

Types of contracts:
- Sealed bid (competitive, fixed price)
- Negotiated (can discuss terms)
- Time and materials (cost-plus)
- Indefinite delivery/quantity (IDIQ)

---

### Getting Started

Step 1: Register with SAM (System for Award Management)
- Free registration
- Unique identifier (DUNS number)
- Tax ID
- Business information
- Annual renewal

Cost: Free
Timeline: Few days to weeks

Step 2: Understand Regulations
- FAR (Federal Acquisition Regulation)
- DFARS (Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement)
- Agency-specific regulations
- Compliance requirements
- Record-keeping standards

Step 3: Certifications
- Small business certification (SBA)
- Women-owned business (WOSB)
- Minority-owned business (MBE)
- Veteran-owned business (VB)
- Service-disabled veteran-owned (SDVOSB)
- HUBZone certified

Benefits: Set-asides, preferences, scoring advantages

---

### Federal Contracting Process

1. Opportunity Identification
- FedBizOpps.gov (now SAM)
- GSA Schedules (pre-negotiated pricing)
- Contracting officer relationships
- Small business development centers

2. Pre-qualification
- Read requirements carefully
- Determine if you can meet them
- Understand evaluation criteria
- Check certifications needed

3. Proposal Preparation
- Technical proposal (how you'll do it)
- Cost proposal (price)
- Past performance (references)
- Certifications and compliance
- Technical approach
- Timeline and resources
- Quality assurance plan

4. Submission
- Follow instructions exactly
- Meet deadlines (no exceptions)
- Submit to correct portal
- Keep records
- Confirmation of receipt

5. Evaluation
- Competitive scoring
- Technical merit reviewed
- Cost reasonableness assessed
- Past performance evaluated
- Scoring criteria applied

6. Award
- Notification of award
- Contract negotiation
- Final terms agreed
- Notice to proceed
- Kickoff meeting

7. Performance
- Meet contract requirements
- Invoice monthly/as specified
- Report on metrics
- Quality assurance
- Compliance maintenance
- Regular communication with CO (Contracting Officer)

---

### Contract Types

Firm Fixed Price (FFP):
- Price set upfront
- No adjustment
- Contractor bears risk
- Most predictable for government
- Needs accurate cost estimates

Cost Plus Fixed Fee (CPFF):
- Reimburse costs + fixed fee
- Less risk for contractor
- Government monitors costs
- Detailed record-keeping required

Time and Materials (T&M):
- Pay for labor + materials
- Used when scope uncertain
- Labor rates negotiated
- Material cost plus markup

---

### Challenges & Solutions

Challenge: Complex compliance
Solution: Hire government contract specialist, use checklist, attend trainings

Challenge: Slow payment (60-90 days typical)
Solution: Invoice promptly, follow procedures, cash flow planning

Challenge: Strict performance requirements
Solution: Hire qualified staff, over-plan, frequent communication with CO

Challenge: Extensive documentation
Solution: Create templates, track everything, organize files

Challenge: Competitive bidding
Solution: Past performance, lower costs, better solutions, relationships

## CANADIAN GOVERNMENT CONTRACTING

### Overview

Federal spending:
- CAD $25+ billion annually in contracts
- Public Services and Procurement Canada (PSPC) main purchaser
- Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED)
- Departments: Defense, Transport, Health, etc.

Provincial/territorial contracts:
- Separate systems by province
- Often smaller but accessible
- Different requirements

---

### Getting Started

Step 1: Register with Buyandsell.gc.ca
- Free registration
- Business information
- Certified account
- Bidder information
- Timeline: Few days

Step 2: Understand Regulations
- Government Contracts Regulations
- Trade agreements (Canadian free trade obligations)
- Procurement policy
- Transparency requirements
- Record-keeping

Step 3: Get Qualified
- Meet technical requirements
- Insurance/bonding if required
- Security clearance (if sensitive)
- Financial stability
- References

---

### Canadian Contracting Process

1. Opportunity Search
- Buyandsell.gc.ca (primary source)
- BidOpportunities.gc.ca (open competitions)
- Agency websites
- Networking

2. Pre-qualification
- Read requirements thoroughly
- Understand evaluation criteria
- Check if capable
- Identify gaps
- Plan response

3. Proposal Submission
- Follow template
- Technical response
- Pricing
- References
- Timeline
- Certifications
- Submit by deadline

4. Evaluation
- Technical scoring
- Cost evaluation
- Past performance
- Weighted criteria
- Transparent process

5. Contract Award
- Winner notification
- Contract signing
- Mobilization
- Kickoff meeting

6. Performance
- Meet deliverables
- Invoice as specified
- Maintain compliance
- Report progress
- Quality assurance

---

### Advantages of Canadian Contracting

- Transparent process (published scoring)
- Smaller contracts often more accessible
- Growing opportunities
- Stable government
- Good payment terms
- Repeat business potential

### Challenges

- Bureaucratic processes
- Compliance complexity
- Competition from established contractors
- French language requirements (federal)
- Slow decision-making

## COMMON STRATEGIES

### Building Capability

Before bidding:
- Build past performance (small projects first)
- Get references
- Develop systems and processes
- Hire qualified staff
- Build reputation
- Start small, grow

---

### Relationship Building

Importance:
- Contracting officers make decisions
- Relationships matter
- Responsiveness valued
- Reliability builds repeat business

Strategies:
- Attend industry conferences
- Meet with contracting officers (pre-bid)
- Join professional associations
- Network with other contractors
- Maintain relationships (even between contracts)

---

### Teaming/Partnerships

Advantage:
- Access larger contracts
- Combine capabilities
- Reduce risk
- Larger team capacity

Teaming agreements:
- Clear roles and responsibilities
- Payment terms between partners
- Performance expectations
- Dispute resolution
- Written agreements

## MISTAKES TO AVOID

1. Not reading requirements: Miss critical details
2. Missing deadlines: Automatic disqualification
3. Unrealistic pricing: Can't perform at that price
4. No past performance: Harder to win
5. Poor proposal: Scoring suffers
6. Non-compliance: Disqualified
7. Bad references: Loses points
8. Overpromising: Can't deliver
9. Ignoring relationships: Miss opportunities
10. Giving up: Takes persistence to win contracts
