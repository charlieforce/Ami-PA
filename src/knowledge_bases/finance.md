# FINANCE & ACCOUNTING EXPERTISE KNOWLEDGE BASE

## FUNDAMENTALS

### Core Concepts

Assets: What company owns (cash, equipment, property)
Liabilities: What company owes (loans, payables, debts)
Equity: Assets minus liabilities (owner's stake)

Accounting Equation:
Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Income Statement: Shows profit/loss over period
Balance Sheet: Shows financial position at point in time
Cash Flow: Shows movement of cash in/out

---

## FINANCIAL STATEMENTS

### Income Statement (P&L)
Shows revenues and expenses over period (month/quarter/year)

Structure:
Revenue: Total money coming in
- Cost of Goods Sold (COGS): Direct production costs
= Gross Profit: Revenue minus COGS

Operating Expenses: Salaries, marketing, rent, etc.
= Operating Income: Profit before interest/taxes

Interest/Other: Interest on debt, other income
= Net Income (Bottom Line): Profit after everything

### Balance Sheet
Shows financial position at specific date

Assets (What you own):
- Current: Cash, receivables, inventory (convertible within year)
- Non-current: Equipment, property, intangibles (longer term)

Liabilities (What you owe):
- Current: Payables, short-term debt (due within year)
- Non-current: Long-term debt, deferred revenue

Equity (Owner's stake):
- Common stock
- Retained earnings
- Preferred stock

### Cash Flow Statement
Shows actual cash movement (critical!)

Operating Cash Flow: Cash from business operations
- Net income
- Plus non-cash expenses (depreciation)
- Minus increases in working capital

Investing Cash Flow: Cash for investments
- Equipment purchases
- Acquisitions
- Asset sales

Financing Cash Flow: Cash from financing
- Debt issuance/repayment
- Equity issuance
- Dividends paid

## KEY FINANCIAL METRICS

### Profitability Ratios

Gross Profit Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue
- Measures production efficiency
- Higher = better
- Example: 60% = $0.60 profit per $1 revenue

Operating Profit Margin = Operating Income / Revenue
- Measures operational efficiency
- Higher = better
- Includes all operating expenses

Net Profit Margin = Net Income / Revenue
- Bottom line profitability
- Higher = better
- Industry varies (SaaS 20-30%, Retail 2-5%)

Return on Assets (ROA) = Net Income / Total Assets
- How efficiently using assets
- Higher = better

Return on Equity (ROE) = Net Income / Equity
- Return to shareholders
- Higher = better
- More leveraged = higher ROE (but riskier)

---

### Liquidity Ratios

Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities
- Can you pay short-term obligations?
- Above 1.5 is healthy
- Below 1.0 is risky

Quick Ratio = (Current Assets - Inventory) / Current Liabilities
- More conservative than current ratio
- Excludes inventory (hard to liquidate)
- Above 1.0 is good

Cash Ratio = Cash / Current Liabilities
- Most conservative
- Can you pay with cash only?
- Varies by industry

---

### Efficiency Ratios

Asset Turnover = Revenue / Total Assets
- How much revenue per dollar of assets
- Higher = more efficient
- Varies by industry

Inventory Turnover = COGS / Average Inventory
- How fast selling inventory
- Higher = better (less capital tied up)
- Varies by industry

Receivables Turnover = Revenue / Average Accounts Receivable
- How fast collecting from customers
- Higher = better
- Related to Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)

---

### Leverage Ratios

Debt-to-Equity = Total Debt / Total Equity
- How much financed by debt vs equity
- Lower = less risky
- Varies by industry (tech 0.5, manufacturing 1.5+)

Debt Ratio = Total Debt / Total Assets
- Percentage financed by debt
- Lower = less risky
- Below 60% is healthy for most

Interest Coverage = Operating Income / Interest Expense
- Can you pay interest on debt?
- Above 2.5 is healthy
- Below 1.0 is danger zone

## STARTUP FINANCIALS

### Key Metrics for Startups

MRR/ARR: Monthly/Annual Recurring Revenue
- Core metric for SaaS
- Predictable revenue stream
- Show growth rate quarter over quarter

Burn Rate: Monthly cash spend
- How fast burning cash
- Runway = Cash / Burn Rate
- Try to improve (lower burn or higher revenue)

Runway: How long until out of cash
- Critical metric
- Usually target 12-18 months
- Plan for fundraising or profitability

CAC: Customer Acquisition Cost
- Total marketing spend / new customers
- Lower = better
- Must be recovered by LTV

LTV: Lifetime Value
- Total revenue expected from customer
- LTV = ARPU * Average Customer Lifespan
- Ratio: LTV:CAC should be 3:1 or better

Burn Multiple = Burn Rate / New ARR Generated
- More efficient = lower multiple
- Top quartile: Under 0.75
- Helps evaluate runway

## BUDGETING & FORECASTING

### Annual Budget Process

1. Revenue forecast: Conservative estimate
   - Historical data
   - Market research
   - Growth assumptions
   - Seasonal adjustments

2. Expense budgeting: By category
   - Headcount (usually 50-70% of costs)
   - Technology and tools
   - Marketing and customer acquisition
   - Operations and overhead
   - Contingency (10-15%)

3. Cash flow forecast: Monthly cash needs
   - Timing of revenue
   - Timing of expenses
   - Tax payments
   - Financing needs

4. Monitoring: Monthly variance
   - Actual vs budget
   - Explain major variances
   - Reforecast if needed
   - Adjust spending if revenue below plan

### Unit Economics

Revenue per unit
- Price
- Volume
- Discounts

Cost per unit
- Direct costs
- Allocated overhead
- Contribution margin

Break-even: Fixed costs / Contribution margin per unit

## PRICING STRATEGY

### Cost-Plus Pricing
Cost + Markup = Price
Example: $100 cost + 100% markup = $200 price

Pros: Simple, ensures margin
Cons: Ignores customer value, ignores competition

### Value-Based Pricing
Price based on value delivered to customer

Example: Software saves customer $500/month
Price: $99-199/month (capture some of the value)

Pros: Higher margins, customer pays for value
Cons: Harder to calculate, variable by customer

### Competitive Pricing
Price based on what competitors charge

Pros: Market validated
Cons: Race to bottom, no differentiation

### Hybrid Approach (Recommended)
Start with cost-plus (floor), add value premium, check against competition

## WORKING CAPITAL MANAGEMENT

### Cash Conversion Cycle
Days to convert cash invested back to cash

Formula:
Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) = Inventory / (COGS / 365)
Plus Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) = Receivables / (Revenue / 365)
Minus Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) = Payables / (COGS / 365)
= Cash Conversion Cycle

Lower = better (cash tied up less time)

### Improving Cash Flow

Accelerate receivables:
- Invoice immediately
- Offer early payment discount
- Stricter credit terms
- Automated collections

Optimize inventory:
- Just-in-time ordering
- Demand forecasting
- Reduce obsolescence
- Sell slow-moving items

Slow payables (carefully):
- Negotiate payment terms
- Use supplier credit
- Plan payments strategically
- Maintain good relationships

## DEBT & EQUITY FINANCING

### Debt Financing
Borrowing money (must be repaid)

Types:
- Bank loans: Fixed rate, fixed term
- Lines of credit: Flexible, interest on amount drawn
- Bonds: Formalized debt, tradeable
- Vendor financing: Deferred payment to suppliers

Pros: Tax deductible interest, no dilution, fixed cost
Cons: Must repay regardless of profit, interest expense, covenants

Debt Covenants: Restrictions lenders impose
- Minimum cash balance
- Maximum debt level
- Profitability requirements
- Capital expenditure limits

### Equity Financing
Selling ownership stake

Types:
- Venture capital: Professional investors
- Angel investors: Individual investors
- Employee stock options: Retain talent
- Strategic investors: Corporate partners

Pros: No repayment required, aligned interests, expertise
Cons: Dilutes ownership, loss of control, pressure for returns

Choosing between debt and equity:
- Early stage with risk: Equity better
- Predictable revenue: Debt okay
- Growth capital: Mix of both
- Mature business: More debt viable

## TAX STRATEGY

### Business Structure Impact

Sole Proprietor: Personal and business income mixed
- Simplest
- But liable for everything
- Not recommended for startups

LLC (Limited Liability Company):
- Liability protection
- Pass-through taxation (individual level)
- Flexible
- Good for small businesses

S-Corp:
- Limited liability
- Lower taxes on distributions
- More complex
- Good if profitable

C-Corp:
- Limited liability
- Double taxation (corporate + individual)
- Standard for VC-backed startups
- SEIS/EIS benefits in UK

### Tax Reduction Strategies

Business expenses:
- Salary to owner
- Employee benefits
- Home office deduction
- Vehicle and travel
- Equipment and software
- Professional services

Timing:
- Bunching expenses in high-income years
- Deferring revenue to low-income years
- Timing of capital gains

Retirement plans:
- 401k (lower taxes, deferred growth)
- SEP-IRA (self-employed)
- Solo 401k (high contribution limits)

## FINANCIAL ANALYSIS

### Trend Analysis
Compare financials over time
- Revenue growth trend
- Margin trends
- Cash position trend
- Identifies patterns and issues

### Comparative Analysis
Compare to competitors or industry averages
- Benchmarking
- Identify performance gaps
- Industry context
- Competitive positioning

### Scenario Analysis
Model different futures
- Best case: High growth, margins improve
- Base case: Expected performance
- Worst case: Economic downturn
- Plan for each scenario

## COMMON FINANCIAL MISTAKES

1. Not tracking metrics: Flying blind
2. Mixing personal and business finances: Confusion and tax issues
3. Not forecasting cash flow: Running out of cash despite profit
4. Growing too fast: Revenue growth with cash drain
5. No contingency: No buffer for unexpected
6. Ignoring working capital: Tied up capital, cash problems
7. Not understanding unit economics: Profitable revenue that loses money
8. Wrong pricing: Too cheap or too expensive
9. No financial controls: Fraud or errors
10. Not reviewing regularly: Problems not caught early
