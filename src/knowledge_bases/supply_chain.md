# SUPPLY CHAIN MANAGEMENT EXPERTISE KNOWLEDGE BASE

## SUPPLY CHAIN FUNDAMENTALS

### What is Supply Chain?
Definition: Network of organizations that move product from supplier to customer

Encompasses:
- Procurement (buying materials)
- Production (making product)
- Logistics (moving product)
- Distribution (getting to customers)
- Returns (handling returns)

Goals:
- Get right product
- To right place
- At right time
- In right condition
- At lowest total cost
- With best service

---

## PROCUREMENT

### Sourcing Strategy

Single Sourcing:
- One supplier only
- Lower costs
- Strong relationship
- Higher risk (supplier failure)

Dual Sourcing:
- Two suppliers
- Some redundancy
- Medium cost
- Better security

Multiple Sourcing:
- Many suppliers
- Most expensive
- Best security
- Competition drives improvements

Preferred Sourcing:
- 1-2 preferred suppliers
- Others for overflow
- Good balance
- Reduces complexity

---

### Supplier Selection

Criteria:
- Cost (price, terms, MOQ)
- Quality (standards, defect rates, certifications)
- Reliability (on-time delivery, communication)
- Capacity (can they scale?)
- Innovation (can they improve?)
- Location (proximity, regulations)
- Financial stability (won't go out of business)
- Compliance (labor, environmental standards)

Evaluation:
- Request for Quote (RFQ)
- Site visits
- Reference checks
- Trial orders
- Quality assessments

---

### Contracts & Terms

Key terms:
- Price and volume discounts
- Delivery terms and frequency
- Quality standards and inspection
- Payment terms and conditions
- Minimum order quantities
- Lead times
- Warranty and returns
- Force majeure clauses
- Termination conditions

Negotiation strategies:
- Volume commitments for discounts
- Payment terms (longer is cheaper)
- Annual vs purchase order (gives certainty)
- Penalty clauses (for late delivery)
- Price escalation clauses (inflation protection)

## INVENTORY MANAGEMENT

### Inventory Types

Raw Materials:
- Materials to make products
- Minimize inventory (costly to store)
- Enough for production schedule

Work-in-Progress (WIP):
- Partially completed products
- Minimize to improve flow
- Too much signals bottleneck

Finished Goods:
- Completed products
- Enough to meet demand
- Too much ties up capital

---

### Inventory Levels

Economic Order Quantity (EOQ):
Balance between:
- Ordering too often (high shipping cost)
- Ordering too much (high storage cost)

Formula: (2 * Demand * Order Cost / Holding Cost) ^ 0.5

Safety Stock:
- Extra inventory for demand spikes
- Protects against stockouts
- Costs money to hold
- Calculate based on variability

Reorder Point:
- Inventory level that triggers new order
- Based on lead time and demand
- Lower = less storage, more risk
- Higher = more storage, less risk

---

### Inventory Optimization

Just-in-Time (JIT):
- Order only when needed
- Minimal inventory
- Fast supplier response required
- High quality critical
- Reduces holding costs
- Increases risk

Lean Inventory:
- Minimal inventory
- Efficient processes
- Regular ordering
- Demand forecasting critical
- Reduces waste
- Lower safety margins

## LOGISTICS & DISTRIBUTION

### Warehousing

Inbound:
- Receiving goods from suppliers
- Quality inspection
- Storing until needed

Outbound:
- Picking orders
- Packing shipments
- Preparing labels
- Handing off to carriers

Costs:
- Facility costs (rent, utilities)
- Labor (staff)
- Equipment (forklifts, shelving)
- Technology (WMS)
- Inventory holding

Optimization:
- Location strategy (near customers, suppliers)
- Layout efficiency (minimize travel)
- Automation (reduces labor)
- Technology (tracking, accuracy)

---

### Transportation

Modes:
- Truck: Flexible, expensive per mile, fast
- Rail: Bulk, cheap per mile, slow
- Ship: Very cheap per ton, very slow
- Air: Most expensive, fastest
- Pipeline: Bulk, specialized, cheap

Selection:
- Cost vs speed tradeoff
- Product type
- Volume
- Distance
- Urgency

Transportation Costs:
- Fuel
- Labor
- Vehicle maintenance
- Tolls and permits
- Insurance

Optimization:
- Full truckload (cheaper per unit)
- Consolidation (combine shipments)
- Route optimization (shorter routes)
- Backhaul optimization (return loads)

---

### Last-Mile Delivery

Definition: Final step from warehouse to customer

Challenges:
- Most expensive (40-60% of total cost)
- Customer expectations (fast, flexible)
- Density (urban cheaper than rural)
- Returns complexity

Options:
- Company-owned fleet
- Third-party carriers (UPS, FedEx, DHL)
- Specialized (Amazon Flex, Instacart)
- Hybrid approach

Optimization:
- Geographic clustering
- Delivery windows
- Consolidation
- Alternative delivery (pickup points)

---

## DEMAND FORECASTING

### Forecasting Methods

Historical Data:
- Look at past sales
- Project into future
- Works if patterns consistent
- Struggles with new products

Qualitative:
- Sales team input
- Customer surveys
- Market research
- Managerial judgment
- Works for new markets

Statistical:
- Time series analysis
- Regression models
- Seasonal adjustments
- Trend analysis
- Requires sufficient data

Collaborative:
- Work with customers
- Share demand forecast
- Adjust together
- More accurate
- Reduce bullwhip effect

---

### Forecast Accuracy

Bullwhip Effect:
- Small changes in consumer demand
- Create larger swings upstream supply chain
- Each level overreacts
- Results in waste and inefficiency

Mitigation:
- Share real demand data
- Reduce variability
- Longer lead time visibility
- Collaborative forecasting
- Vendor-managed inventory

## SUPPLY CHAIN METRICS

### Efficiency Metrics

Perfect Order Rate:
- Orders delivered on-time, complete, damage-free
- Higher = better
- Industry target: 95%+

Order Cycle Time:
- Days from order to delivery
- Lower = better
- Competitive advantage

Inventory Turnover:
- Cost of goods sold / Average inventory
- Higher = more efficient
- Avoid too high (stockouts)

Cost per Unit:
- Total supply chain cost / Units
- Lower = better
- Continuous improvement focus

---

### Quality Metrics

Defect Rate:
- % of products with defects
- Lower = better
- Cost of quality

Return Rate:
- % of sold products returned
- Lower = better
- Indicates quality

Supplier Quality Rating:
- Defect rates, on-time delivery, responsiveness
- Holds suppliers accountable
- Drives improvements

---

### Responsiveness Metrics

Order Accuracy:
- % of orders with correct items
- Higher = better
- Reflects operational excellence

Delivery Performance:
- % of on-time deliveries
- Higher = better
- Customer satisfaction

Forecast Accuracy:
- How close forecast to actual
- Higher = better
- Enables efficiency

## COMMON SUPPLY CHAIN MISTAKES

1. Ignoring costs: Penny wise, pound foolish
2. Single source: No redundancy
3. No forecasting: Guessing leads to inefficiency
4. Poor supplier relationships: Transactional, not strategic
5. Technology gaps: Manual processes, errors
6. Ignoring risks: Disruptions catch you unprepared
7. No metrics: Can't manage what you can't measure
8. Over-inventory: Wastes capital
9. Under-inventory: Stockouts and lost sales
10. Not collaborating: Bullwhip effect increases costs
