# ELECTRICAL SYSTEMS EXPERTISE KNOWLEDGE BASE

## FUNDAMENTALS

### Basic Concepts

Voltage (V): Electrical pressure, measured in Volts
- Household: 120V (US), 230V (Europe/Africa)
- Devices marked: 110-240V works worldwide

Current (I): Flow of electricity, measured in Amps (A)
- Higher current = more power
- Typical circuit: 15-20A

Resistance (R): Opposition to current, measured in Ohms
- Higher resistance = less current
- Heating elements are high resistance

Power (W): Energy consumption, measured in Watts
- Formula: Watts = Volts times Amps
- Typical outlet: 120V times 15A = 1800W max

## HOME ELECTRICAL SYSTEM

### Main Components

Electrical Panel (Breaker Box):
- Central hub of home electrical system
- Circuit breakers protect circuits
- Main breaker shuts off all power
- Location: Usually basement or garage

Circuits:
- 120V circuits: Lighting, outlets (15-20A)
- 240V circuits: AC, dryer, water heater (30-50A)
- Ground/Neutral: Return path for electricity
- Multiple outlets on one circuit (not recommended to overload)

Wiring:
- Wire gauge: Thickness (larger = higher capacity)
- Common: 14 AWG (15A), 12 AWG (20A), 10 AWG (30A)
- Color coding:
  - Black: Hot (live power)
  - White: Neutral (return)
  - Red: Hot (second circuit)
  - Green/Bare copper: Ground (safety)

Outlets & Switches:
- Standard outlet: 15A, 120V
- Heavy-duty outlet: 20A, 120V
- 240V outlet: AC, dryer, range
- GFCI (Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter): Bathroom/kitchen safety (shuts off if detects leak)
- Switches: Control power to lights/outlets

## COMMON ELECTRICAL PROBLEMS

### Problem 1: Tripped Circuit Breaker

What happens: Breaker flips to OFF, outlet loses power

Causes:
- Too many devices on one circuit (overload)
- Short circuit (damaged wire)
- Faulty appliance

Diagnosis:
1. Note which breaker tripped
2. Unplug devices on that circuit
3. Reset breaker (flip to ON)
4. If it keeps tripping: Problem with circuit, not load

Solutions:
- If load overload: Spread devices across circuits
- If keeps tripping: Check for damaged wires or faulty appliance
- Professional: Call electrician if recurring

---

### Problem 2: Dead Outlets

What happens: Outlet provides no power

Causes:
- Tripped circuit breaker
- Blown fuse (old systems)
- Faulty outlet
- Damaged wiring

Diagnosis:
1. Check if other outlets on same circuit work
2. Check circuit breaker status
3. Try different device
4. Look for damage near outlet

Solutions:
- Breaker tripped: Reset it (see above)
- One outlet dead: Likely faulty outlet, replace it ($15-30)
- Multiple outlets: Breaker or wiring issue
- Professional: Call if DIY doesn't work

---

### Problem 3: Flickering Lights

What happens: Lights flicker on and off

Causes:
- Loose bulb
- Loose connection
- Faulty switch
- High load starting (appliances)
- Utility company issue

Diagnosis:
1. Try tightening bulb first
2. Does it flicker with specific appliance?
3. Does whole house flicker or one light?
4. Is connection loose?

Solutions:
- Loose bulb: Tighten it
- Single light: Try new bulb or check switch
- Multiple lights: Could be utility issue
- Professional: If bulb/switch change doesn't help

---

### Problem 4: Burning Smell from Outlet

CRITICAL - SAFETY HAZARD

What it means: Electrical fire risk

Immediate action:
1. STOP - Do not use outlet
2. Turn off breaker to that circuit
3. Do not touch outlet
4. Call electrician IMMEDIATELY
5. Consider leaving home if smell persists

What causes it:
- Wire insulation burning
- Loose connection heating up
- Faulty appliance
- Old, overused outlet

Do NOT:
- Ignore it
- Try to fix it yourself
- Plug anything else in
- Wait to call professional

## ELECTRICAL SAFETY

### Golden Rules
1. Electricity kills: Respect it, don't take chances
2. Always use proper tools: Insulated screwdrivers, voltage tester
3. Turn off power before working: Even small jobs
4. Never work wet: Water + electricity = electrocution
5. Test before touching: Use voltage tester ($10-20)
6. Call professional: If unsure, get expert

### Safe Practices
- Keep cords away from water
- Don't overload outlets (use 1 device per outlet if possible)
- Don't run cords under rugs (fire hazard)
- Don't use damaged cords
- Use surge protectors for electronics
- GFCI outlets in wet areas (bathroom, kitchen)
- Keep water 6+ feet from outlets

## BASIC TROUBLESHOOTING

### When to DIY
- Flip breaker to reset
- Tighten loose bulbs
- Replace outlet (if comfortable)
- Visual inspection for damage

### When to Call Professional
- Persistent flickering
- Burning smell
- Repeated tripping
- Unusual shocks
- Any time unsure
- Installation work
- Upgrades/additions

### Cost Expectations
- Service call: $75-150
- Simple fix: $150-300
- Outlet replacement: $100-200
- Breaker replacement: $200-500
- Major rewiring: $1,000+

## MAINTENANCE

### Regular Checks
- Monthly: Test GFCI outlets (press test button)
- Quarterly: Visual inspection for damage
- Yearly: Professional inspection if old house

### Warning Signs
- Burning smell
- Shocks from outlets
- Flickering lights
- Hot outlet covers
- Buzzing sounds
- Discolored outlets/switches

### Preventive Maintenance
- Don't overload circuits
- Keep outlets clean
- Update old wiring (houses over 30 years)
- Upgrade GFCI protection
- Regular inspections

## ELECTRICAL CODES & STANDARDS

### Why They Matter
- Safety requirements
- Prevent fires
- Standardized practices
- Insurance/selling house
- Inspection compliance

### Common Codes
- GFCI in kitchens/bathrooms: Required
- Grounding: All outlets should be 3-prong
- Arc Fault Protection: Bedrooms require AFCI
- Spacing: Outlets max 6 feet apart in living areas
- Wire gauge: Must match breaker size
