# NON PROFIT ORGANIZATION IN AFRICA EXPERTISE KNOWLEDGE BASE

## FUNDAMENTALS

### What is a Non-Profit Organization?

Definition: Organization serving social mission, not profit distribution

Characteristics:
- Tax-exempt status (in most jurisdictions)
- Mission-driven (social impact)
- No private shareholders
- Surplus reinvested in mission
- Board governance
- Public accountability
- Donor/grant funding

---

## AFRICAN CONTEXT

### Unique Challenges in Africa

Infrastructure:
- Unreliable electricity (solar backup needed)
- Limited internet connectivity
- Poor road infrastructure
- Limited banking access
- Currency volatility

Funding:
- Limited local philanthropy
- Dependent on international donors
- Government funding scarce
- Competition for same donors
- Exchange rate risks

Regulatory:
- Inconsistent regulations across countries
- Weak enforcement
- Corruption risks
- Bureaucratic delays
- Limited donor protection laws

Staffing:
- Limited skilled professionals
- High staff turnover
- Brain drain to developed countries
- Limited training opportunities
- Salary pressures

## MISSION & THEORY OF CHANGE

### Defining Mission

Clear mission statement:
- What problem you solve
- Who you serve
- How you create impact
- Long-term vision
- Measurable outcomes

Example mission:
"Empower 10,000 Sierra Leonean women with vocational training and business skills to create sustainable livelihoods"

### Theory of Change

Logic model:
- Inputs: Resources, funding, staff, volunteers
- Activities: What you do (training, counseling, services)
- Outputs: Deliverables (# trained, # served)
- Outcomes: Changes in participants (skills, income, health)
- Impact: System-level change (reduced poverty, improved community)

Importance:
- Guides strategy
- Measures effectiveness
- Communicates to donors
- Enables learning and iteration

## ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE

### Board of Directors

Role:
- Governance and oversight
- Strategic direction
- Fundraising
- Legal accountability
- Hiring executive director

Composition:
- Chair: Leads board
- Treasurer: Financial oversight
- Secretary: Records and compliance
- Members: 3-15 typically

Diversity needed:
- Sector expertise (education, health, business)
- Geographic diversity
- Gender balance
- Lived experience of issue
- Fundraising connections

Responsibilities:
- Monthly/quarterly meetings
- Committee participation
- Donor relations
- Strategic planning
- Accountability and oversight

---

### Staff Structure

Typical structure:
- Executive Director: Overall leadership
- Program Manager: Manage programs
- Finance Manager: Money and reporting
- Communications: Storytelling and marketing
- Support staff: Administration, logistics

Sizing:
- Start lean (founder-led)
- Hire as revenue grows
- Avoid bloated overhead (donors watch)
- Typical 15-20% overhead

## PROGRAMS & ACTIVITIES

### Program Design

Needs assessment:
- Who has the problem?
- How severe is problem?
- What interventions work?
- What's missing in market?
- Where are gaps?

Program types:
- Education: Scholarships, training, schools
- Health: Clinics, maternal health, disease prevention
- Economic: Microfinance, vocational training, job placement
- Social: Counseling, community building, advocacy
- Environmental: Reforestation, conservation, climate

---

### Monitoring & Evaluation

Importance:
- Proves impact
- Informs improvement
- Justifies donor funding
- Builds credibility

Metrics:
- Output metrics: # trained, # served, # events
- Outcome metrics: Skills gained, income increase, health improvement
- Impact metrics: Community-level change

Data collection:
- Baseline (before program)
- Regular tracking (during program)
- Endline (after program)
- Qualitative stories
- Quantitative data

## FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

### Budgeting

Annual budget process:
1. Review past year expenses
2. Forecast revenue
3. Plan programs and costs
4. Allocate resources
5. Build contingency

Budget categories:
- Program costs (60-70%)
- Personnel (20-30%)
- Operations (5-10%)
- Overhead (10-20%)

Donor expectations:
- Program spending 70%+
- Overhead 30% or less
- Transparency in spending
- Regular financial reports

---

### Financial Controls

Importance:
- Prevent fraud
- Ensure accuracy
- Build donor trust
- Enable compliance

Controls:
- Segregation of duties (no one person controls all money)
- Approval processes (multiple people sign off)
- Regular reconciliation
- External audit
- Documentation

Banking:
- Separate organizational account
- Multiple signatories
- Regular statements
- Clear records

## CHALLENGES & SOLUTIONS

### Common Challenges

Funding:
- Unpredictable donor support
- Competition for funding
- Dependency on few donors
- Solution: Diversify funding, build reserves, evidence of impact

Sustainability:
- Can't maintain programs long-term
- Staff turnover
- Solution: Social enterprises, government contracts, endowment

Impact measurement:
- Hard to prove causation
- Limited data capacity
- Solution: Theory of change, regular evaluation, partner with universities

Staff capacity:
- Limited skilled professionals
- Brain drain
- Solution: Training, leadership development, competitive salaries

## BEST PRACTICES

1. Clear mission: Everyone aligned
2. Strong board: Governance and accountability
3. Proven model: Evidence of what works
4. Financial discipline: Trust and sustainability
5. Impact focus: Measure and improve
6. Transparency: Donor and public trust
7. Local ownership: Community buy-in
8. Partnerships: Leverage others' expertise
9. Sustainability planning: Beyond founder
10. Continuous learning: Adapt and improve
