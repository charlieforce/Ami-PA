# SMALL BUSINESS (USA) EXPERTISE KNOWLEDGE BASE

## USA SMALL BUSINESS CONTEXT

### Definition

Small business (SBA definition):
- Fewer than 500 employees (typical)
- Fewer than $7.5-38.5 million in revenue (varies by industry)
- Independent
- Not dominant in its field

Statistics:
- 28 million small businesses in USA
- 60+ million employed by small business
- 44% of economic activity
- High growth potential
- High failure rate (20% fail first year)

## LEGAL STRUCTURE

### Sole Proprietorship

What: Individual operates business

Pros:
- Simplest to start
- Full control
- All profit yours
- Easy to close

Cons:
- Personal liability (sued personally)
- Unlimited liability
- Hard to raise capital
- No separation business/personal

Best for: Solo freelancers, service providers

---

### LLC (Limited Liability Company)

What: Separate legal entity

Pros:
- Limited liability (personal assets protected)
- Pass-through taxation
- Flexibility
- Relatively simple
- Can have multiple owners

Cons:
- Annual filings and fees
- Self-employment taxes
- Limited life (dies with owners in some states)
- Less formal than corporation

Best for: Most small businesses

Cost: $50-500 to form, $50-200 annual renewal

---

### S-Corporation

What: C-Corp taxed as partnership

Pros:
- Limited liability
- Tax savings (if profitable)
- Professional image
- Easier to raise capital

Cons:
- Complex compliance
- Expensive to form and maintain
- Formal requirements
- Restrictions on shareholders

Best for: Profitable businesses wanting to save on taxes

---

### C-Corporation

What: Separate legal entity

Pros:
- Strong liability protection
- Easier to raise capital
- Perpetual existence
- Professional image

Cons:
- Double taxation
- Complex compliance
- Expensive to form and maintain
- More formal requirements

Best for: Venture-backed startups, planning to sell/go public

---

## FINANCING OPTIONS

### Self-Funding (Bootstrap)

Use personal savings
- No debt or equity dilution
- Full control
- Limited capital
- Slower growth

Typical: $5,000-50,000 initial investment

---

### Small Business Administration (SBA) Loans

Government-backed loans
- Easier to qualify than banks
- Lower interest rates
- Longer repayment terms
- Still require business plan and collateral

Types:
- 7(a) loan program (up to $5 million)
- Microloan program (up to $50,000)
- Express loans (faster)

Terms: 5-25 years, rates 7-12%, fees 1-3%

---

### Bank Loans

Traditional bank financing
- Higher rates than SBA
- Requires good credit
- Collateral required
- Faster than SBA
- More stringent requirements

---

### Credit Cards

Flexible financing
- Immediate access
- High interest rates (15-25%)
- Revolving credit
- Be careful of debt trap

---

### Friends and Family

Informal investor
- Flexible terms
- Relationship-based
- Document everything (even informal)
- Clear expectations prevent conflict

---

### Angel Investors

Individuals investing in startups
- Equity stake
- Mentoring often included
- Larger amounts than friends/family
- Exit expectations (want return)
- Network opportunities

---

### Venture Capital

Professional investors
- Large investments ($500K-millions)
- Equity stake
- Board involvement
- Exit expectations (acquisition/IPO)
- High growth expectations
- Loss of control risk

---

## BUSINESS REGISTRATION & COMPLIANCE

### Federal Registration

EIN (Employer Identification Number):
- Free from IRS
- Apply online
- Immediate if online
- Needed for taxes, hiring, banking

---

### State Registration

Varies by state:
- File articles of organization/incorporation
- Registered agent
- Annual reports
- Renewal fees
- Compliance calendar

Cost: $50-500 depending on state

---

### Local Requirements

Varies by city/county:
- Business license
- Zoning compliance
- Health permits (food business)
- Professional licenses
- Building permits

---

### Employer Identification

If hiring:
- Verify eligibility (I-9 forms)
- Provide tax documents (W-4)
- Withhold taxes
- Pay payroll taxes quarterly
- File annual reports

---

### Tax Obligations

Federal:
- Quarterly estimated taxes (if profit expected)
- Annual income tax return
- Self-employment tax (if self-employed)
- Form 1099 if using contractors

State/Local:
- State income tax (if applicable)
- Sales tax (if applicable)
- Payroll taxes (if employees)
- Property tax (if property owned)

## COMMON SMALL BUSINESS CHALLENGES

### Challenge 1: Undercapitalization

Problem: Not enough cash to start/grow

Solutions:
- Start small (bootstrap)
- Seek business loans
- Partner/investor for capital
- Delay expansion
- Improve cash flow

---

### Challenge 2: Cash Flow

Problem: Revenue not enough to pay bills

Solutions:
- Invoice immediately
- Require deposits/prepayment
- Offer early payment discount
- Negotiate payment terms with vendors
- Build cash reserves
- Line of credit backup

---

### Challenge 3: Hiring & Staff

Problem: Finding and keeping quality staff

Solutions:
- Competitive wages
- Training investment
- Flexible arrangements
- Growth opportunities
- Positive culture
- Performance-based incentives

---

### Challenge 4: Market Competition

Problem: Too many competitors

Solutions:
- Specialization (narrow focus)
- Quality focus
- Better customer service
- Lower costs
- Unique value proposition
- Niche market

---

### Challenge 5: Time Management

Problem: Wearing too many hats

Solutions:
- Hire help early
- Delegate responsibilities
- Automate where possible
- Focus on high-value activities
- Take breaks/avoid burnout
- Prioritize ruthlessly

---

## GROWTH STRATEGIES

### Organic Growth

Expand with existing resources:
- Increase marketing
- Improve customer service
- Expand product/service line
- New customer acquisition
- Referral programs
- Cheaper and slower

---

### Acquisition Growth

Buy other businesses:
- Immediate scale
- Proven business
- Existing customers
- Faster growth
- Requires capital
- Integration challenges

---

### Franchising

Expand through franchise model:
- Others open locations
- Royalty income
- Your brand grows
- Less capital intensive
- Less control
- Franchisor responsibilities

---

### Strategic Partnerships

Partner with complementary businesses:
- Cross-promotion
- Share resources
- Access new markets
- Win-win arrangements
- Clear agreements needed

## BUSINESS METRICS TO TRACK

### Financial

- Revenue and growth rate
- Profit margin
- Cash flow
- Burn rate (if spending savings)
- Runway (time to profitability)
- Unit economics

### Customer

- Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
- Customer lifetime value (LTV)
- Churn rate
- Retention rate
- Net Promoter Score (NPS)
- Customer satisfaction

### Operational

- Productivity per employee
- Customer satisfaction
- Quality metrics
- Efficiency metrics
- Time to deliver
- Cost per unit

## COMMON MISTAKES

1. Undercapitalization: Start with too little money
2. Poor planning: No business plan
3. No market research: Don't know customer
4. Hiring wrong people: Bad team culture
5. Poor accounting: Don't track finances
6. Ignoring competition: Don't know market
7. Over-expansion: Grow too fast
8. Poor customer service: Lose customers
9. Founder burnout: No breaks, work too hard
10. Giving up early: Don't persist through rough patches
