# GRANTS FOR NON PROFIT ORGANIZATIONS KNOWLEDGE BASE

## GRANT FUNDAMENTALS

### What is a Grant?

Definition: Money given to non-profit for specific purpose, no repayment required

Characteristics:
- Free money (no repayment)
- Competitive process
- Specific purpose (restricted)
- Reporting required
- Application process

Grant vs Loan:
- Grant: Free, no repayment
- Loan: Must repay with interest
- Loan: Easier to get, costs money
- Grant: Harder to get, free

---

## TYPES OF GRANTS

### Foundation Grants

What: Private/family foundations give grants

Foundation types:
- Private foundations (established by individual/family)
- Corporate foundations (company-sponsored)
- Community foundations (local focus)
- Operating foundations (run their own programs)

Characteristics:
- Moderate to large amounts ($10K-millions)
- Specific giving priorities
- Multi-year possible
- Requires relationship building
- Higher success rate if aligned

Finding foundations:
- Foundation Center database
- Guidestar.org
- Local foundations research
- Prospect research
- Networking

---

### Government Grants

What: Government agencies award grants

Types:
- Federal grants
- State grants
- Local/city grants
- Grant programs vary by agency

Characteristics:
- Large amounts possible
- Very competitive
- Complex application
- Detailed reporting required
- Long decision timeline (3-6 months)

Finding government grants:
- Grants.gov (federal)
- Federal agency websites
- State/local websites
- Catalog of Federal Domestic Assistance (CFDA)

---

### Corporate Grants

What: Companies give grants as part of CSR (Corporate Social Responsibility)

Characteristics:
- Company priorities matter (brand alignment)
- Smaller amounts typically
- Local focus common
- Employee involvement valued
- Local presence helps

Finding:
- Company websites (search "grants" or "foundation")
- Local business chamber
- Corporate giving directories
- Direct relationship-building

---

### Individual Donors/Major Gifts

What: Individual people give substantial money

Different than grants:
- More personal relationship
- Flexible than grants
- Tax-deductible
- Recognition opportunities
- Recurring possible

Finding/cultivating:
- Wealth screening
- Networking
- Event cultivation
- Donor database
- Personal ask (most successful)

## GRANT LIFECYCLE

### Step 1: Research & Qualification

Identify grant opportunities:
- Foundation priorities align with your mission
- Funding level appropriate
- Geographic match
- Population/issue match
- Timing works

Assessment:
- Can you meet requirements?
- Do you have capacity?
- Is it worth effort?
- What's success probability?
- When is deadline?

---

### Step 2: Relationship Building

Before applying (especially foundations):
- Research the funder
- Contact to learn more
- Share about your organization
- Ask if good fit
- Get feedback
- Build relationship

---

### Step 3: Proposal Development

Proposal components:

Executive Summary (1 page):
- Who you are
- What you do
- What you're asking for
- Impact

Statement of Need (1-2 pages):
- Problem you're addressing
- Who affected
- Why it matters
- Current gaps
- Research/data

Project Description (2-3 pages):
- What you'll do specifically
- Activities and timeline
- Who will run it
- How will you monitor progress
- Theory of change/logic model

Goals and Objectives (1 page):
- Specific, measurable goals
- Timeline
- Success metrics
- Who benefits

Budget and Budget Narrative:
- Detailed budget breakdown
- Justification for expenses
- Realistic assumptions
- Cost-effectiveness

Organizational Background (1-2 pages):
- Mission and history
- Board/staff
- Track record
- Capacity
- Financial stability

Evaluation Plan:
- How you'll measure success
- Data collection methods
- Timeline
- Who responsible
- How results will be used

---

### Step 4: Proposal Writing Tips

Clarity:
- Simple language (avoid jargon)
- Well-organized
- Easy to scan
- Compelling writing
- Tell a story

Alignment:
- Match funder priorities
- Use their language
- Show understanding
- Demonstrate fit
- Address their concerns

Strength:
- Clear need
- Proven ability to solve
- Realistic plan
- Strong team
- Compelling outcomes

Length:
- Follow funder guidelines exactly
- Usually 10-15 pages
- May include appendix (letters, data, resumes)

---

### Step 5: Budget Development

Categories:
- Personnel (salaries, benefits)
- Program costs (direct costs)
- Operations (overhead, rent, utilities)
- Equipment (if under $5K typically)
- Evaluation (data collection, monitoring)
- Travel
- Other expenses

Realistic:
- Based on actual costs
- Account for inflation
- Include all costs
- Don't lowball (can't deliver)
- Don't inflate (lose credibility)

Funder expectations:
- Program/overhead ratio (usually 70/30 or 80/20)
- Cost-effectiveness
- Value for money
- No excessive salaries
- Reasonable equipment

---

### Step 6: Submission

Follow instructions exactly:
- Submit before deadline
- Use required format
- Answer all questions
- Include all attachments
- Proofread carefully

Deadline:
- No exceptions
- Submit early (avoid technical issues)
- Keep copy for records
- Track where submitted

---

### Step 7: Follow-Up and Decision

Timeline:
- Funders take 1-3 months typically
- Some phone follow-up ok (ask guidelines)
- Don't pester
- Be patient
- Multiple submissions ok (don't put all eggs in one basket)

Decision:
- Notification by mail/email
- Some require meeting
- Congratulations or rejection
- Learn from rejection

---

### Step 8: Grant Management

If awarded:
- Comply with all requirements
- Track spending carefully
- Document outcomes
- Report on schedule
- Communicate with funder
- Build relationship for future

Reporting:
- Financial report (how money spent)
- Program report (outcomes achieved)
- Narrative (what happened, learned, challenges)
- Photos/testimonials
- Evaluation results

## COMMON GRANT MISTAKES

1. No alignment: Applying for irrelevant grants
2. Poor writing: Unclear, unconvincing proposal
3. Weak need: Don't prove why this matters
4. Overpromising: Can't achieve stated goals
5. No evaluation: Don't prove outcomes
6. Missing deadline: Automatic rejection
7. Bad budget: Unrealistic or incomplete
8. No follow-up: Miss relationship opportunity
9. One grant strategy: Only applying to one funder
10. Weak evaluation: Can't prove you succeeded

---

## SUCCESS TIPS

1. Diversify: Multiple funders reduce risk
2. Relationship: Build funder relationships before asking
3. Track record: Show past success
4. Clarity: Crystal clear what you're doing and why
5. Outcomes: Prove you'll measure success
6. Sustainability: Show how project continues
7. Team: Strong team increases confidence
8. Multiple asks: Pipeline of proposals
9. Learning: Apply lessons from rejections
10. Patience: Grant funding takes time
