## ANATOMY & PHYSIOLOGY

### Female Anatomy

External (vulva):
- Labia majora/minora (outer/inner lips)
- Clitoris (pleasure center, thousands nerve endings)
- Vaginal opening
- Perineum (area between vagina and anus)

Internal:
- Vagina (elastic canal)
- Cervix (opening to uterus)
- Uterus (womb)
- Fallopian tubes
- Ovaries (produce eggs and hormones)

G-spot:
- Sensitive area inside vagina
- Varies by person (not everyone has sensitive one)
- Different sensation than clitoral stimulation

---

### Male Anatomy

External:
- Penis (shaft and glans/head)
- Foreskin (retracts, varies by circumcision)
- Scrotum (holds testicles)
- Perineum (area behind scrotum)

Internal:
- Testicles (produce sperm and testosterone)
- Prostate gland (produces fluid in ejaculate)
- Seminal vesicles (contribute to ejaculate)
- Epididymis (stores sperm)
- Vas deferens (transports sperm)

Prostate:
- Walnut-sized gland
- Produces seminal fluid
- Sensitive, can provide pleasure
- Health important (prostate cancer risk)

## SEXUAL RESPONSE CYCLE

### Stages (Both Genders)

Stage 1: Desire/Arousal
- Mental excitement
- Blood flow increases
- Erection/lubrication
- Heart rate rises
- Skin flushing

Stage 2: Plateau
- Sustained arousal
- Increased muscle tension
- Continued blood flow
- Building intensity

Stage 3: Orgasm
- Rhythmic muscle contractions
- Release of sexual tension
- Involuntary response
- Varies in intensity and duration
- Different experience for everyone

Stage 4: Resolution
- Relaxation
- Heart rate normalization
- Skin cooling
- Emotional connection
- Refractory period (men typically need recovery time)

## SEXUAL DYSFUNCTION

### Desire Issues

Low desire:
- Medical (hormones, medications)
- Psychological (stress, depression)
- Relational (disconnect, resentment)
- Lifestyle (exhaustion, poor health)

Solutions:
- Medical evaluation
- Address underlying issues
- Counseling if psychological
- Relationship work
- Lifestyle changes

---

### Arousal Issues

Female:
- Difficulty with lubrication
- Arousal takes longer
- Medical (hormones, medications)
- Psychological (anxiety, trauma)

Male:
- Erectile dysfunction (ED)
- Difficulty maintaining erection
- Medical (cardiovascular, diabetes)
- Psychological (performance anxiety)

Solutions:
- Medical evaluation
- Viagra/similar (temporary fix)
- Address underlying issues
- Therapy
- Lifestyle changes (exercise, stress reduction)

---

### Orgasm Issues

Female:
- Difficulty reaching orgasm
- Requires specific stimulation
- Clitoral vs vaginal debate
- Psychological blocks common
- Most don't orgasm from penetration alone

Male:
- Premature ejaculation (too quickly)
- Delayed ejaculation (takes too long)
- Anorgasmia (can't reach)

Solutions:
- Communication with partner
- Understanding own body
- Techniques and practice
- Addressing psychological factors
- Professional help if persistent

## SEXUAL HEALTH & WELLNESS

### Safer Sex

STI Prevention:
- Condoms (most effective barrier)
- Regular testing
- Communication with partners
- Monogamy if agreed
- Vaccination (HPV, Hepatitis B)

Birth Control:
- Variety of options (condoms, pills, IUDs, etc.)
- Effectiveness varies
- Discussed with partner and healthcare
- Backup methods if worried
- Access and education important

Regular checkups:
- Annual sexual health screening
- STI testing if sexually active
- Cancer screenings (cervical, prostate)
- Sexual health discussion with doctor

---

### Sexual Health Across Lifespan

Adolescence:
- Hormonal changes
- First experiences often confusing
- Education and support important
- Consent teaching critical
- Safe exploration

Young adults:
- Exploration and pleasure
- Relationship development
- Fertility questions
- Contraception decisions

Mid-life:
- Continued intimacy important
- Hormonal changes (menopause/andropause)
- Potential decline in frequency
- Still satisfying possible
- Open communication needed

Older adults:
- Sexual activity continues
- Adaptation to changes
- Medications affect function
- Continued intimacy important
- Health issues addressed

## COMMUNICATION & CONSENT

### Consent

Definition:
- Freely given agreement
- Ongoing (can withdraw anytime)
- Informed (know what agreeing to)
- Enthusiastic (not pressured)
- Specific (yes to one thing ≠ yes to all)

Importance:
- Legal requirement
- Ethical requirement
- Enhances pleasure
- Builds trust
- Prevents harm

Red flags:
- Pressure or coercion
- Intoxication/incapacity
- Not enthusiastic
- Lack of clarity
- History of violation

---

### Talking About Sex

Importance:
- Partner needs to know preferences
- Builds intimacy
- Improves satisfaction
- Addresses concerns
- Prevents misunderstandings

How to start:
- Private, calm setting
- Non-judgmental tone
- Honest and vulnerable
- Listen without defensiveness
- Ask questions
- Regularly check in

What to discuss:
- Desires and fantasies
- Boundaries and limits
- Frequency preferences
- Birth control
- STI status/testing
- Performance concerns
- Relationship changes

## SEXUAL HEALTH PROBLEMS SEEKING HELP

### When to Seek Professional Help

See doctor if:
- Pain during sex
- Erectile dysfunction
- Inability to reach orgasm
- Low sexual desire
- Concern about health

See therapist/counselor if:
- Trauma/abuse history
- Relationship conflict about sex
- Sexual anxiety
- Shame or guilt
- Communication challenges
- Compulsive behaviors

Resources:
- Sexual health clinics
- Therapists specializing in sex
- Books and education
- Online communities (reputable)

## MYTHS & MISCONCEPTIONS

### Common False Beliefs

Myth: Size matters (determines pleasure)
Reality: Size less important than skill, communication, emotional connection

Myth: More sex = healthier relationship
Reality: Quality and mutual satisfaction matter, frequency varies

Myth: Men always want sex
Reality: Sexual desire varies by individual regardless of gender

Myth: Women need to be in love first
Reality: Women can enjoy casual sex; individuals vary

Myth: Orgasm required for good sex
Reality: Pleasure and connection valued equally

Myth: Women have penis envy
Reality: Mostly psychological theory, individual experience varies
