# SALES TECHNIQUES EXPERTISE KNOWLEDGE BASE

## SALES FUNDAMENTALS

### What is Sales?
Definition: Guiding prospects from interest to purchase

Not: Pushy, manipulative, tricky
Yes: Consultative, solution-focused, honest

Sales mindset:
- Help prospect solve problem
- Understand their real needs
- Recommend best solution (even if not yours)
- Build long-term relationship
- Win based on value, not tricks

## SALES PROCESS

### Stage 1: Prospecting
Finding potential customers

Techniques:
- Cold outreach (email, calls, LinkedIn)
- Referrals (ask satisfied customers)
- Warm introductions (mutual connections)
- Content-driven (inbound via valuable content)
- Events (conferences, webinars)
- Partnerships (partner referrals)

Tips:
- Target right personas
- Personalization matters
- Small ask first (not demo immediately)
- Follow up consistently
- Track metrics (response rate, meetings booked)

### Stage 2: Qualification
Determine if they're good fit

Questions to ask:
- Do they have the problem?
- Do they have budget?
- Do they have authority?
- Do they have timeline?
- Is there urgency?

BANT Framework:
- Budget: Do they have money?
- Authority: Can they decide?
- Need: Do they have the problem?
- Timeline: When do they need it?

If any is missing: Might not be right prospect right now

### Stage 3: Discovery
Understand their needs deeply

Goals:
- Understand current situation
- Identify pain points
- Discover desired outcome
- Uncover obstacles
- Find buying criteria

Discovery questions:
- Tell me about your current situation
- What's working well?
- What's not working?
- What would ideal look like?
- What's preventing you from fixing this?
- How much could you save/gain if solved?
- Who else is involved in decision?

Listen more than talk:
- 70% listening, 30% talking
- Take notes
- Ask follow-up questions
- Summarize to confirm understanding

### Stage 4: Proposal
Present solution aligned to their needs

Structure:
1. Recap their situation (show you listened)
2. Challenges they face (pain points)
3. Your solution (how you solve it)
4. Why you over alternatives (differentiation)
5. Investment and ROI (cost and benefit)
6. Next steps (clear path forward)

Proposal document:
- Executive summary (one page)
- Situation and challenges
- Proposed solution
- Timeline and milestones
- Investment (price)
- Expected outcomes
- Terms and conditions

Make it easy to say yes:
- Clear pricing (no hidden fees)
- Flexible payment terms
- Implementation support
- Success metrics defined

### Stage 5: Negotiation
Work toward agreement

Win-win negotiation:
- Understand their constraints
- Understand your constraints
- Find creative solutions
- Both better off than starting

Common requests:
- Price reduction: Offer instead longer contract, added services, implementation support
- Extended trial: Agree, but set success criteria for conversion
- Custom features: Scope carefully, may not be priority for you
- Payment terms: Extended payment versus upfront discount

Never:
- Discount to win (sets wrong expectation)
- Make promises you can't keep
- Bad-mouth competitors
- Pressure or manipulate

### Stage 6: Closing
Get the signature

Types of closes:
- Assumptive: "Let's get started on implementation Tuesday"
- Alternative: "Would you prefer monthly or annual?"
- Direct: "Shall we move forward?"
- Urgency: Time-limited offer (but honest)

Signs they're ready:
- Asking implementation details
- Timeline questions
- User questions
- Budget questions
- Contract questions

If not ready:
- Don't force it
- Understand objection
- Address concern
- Suggest next step

### Stage 7: Implementation & Onboarding
Start strong with customer

Critical for:
- First value realization (aha moment)
- Success (not just activation)
- Expansion (upsell opportunities)
- Retention (customer lifetime value)

## OBJECTION HANDLING

### Common Objections

"It's too expensive"
- Reframe: Total cost of ownership (including status quo cost)
- ROI: Show financial benefit
- Alternative: Lower tier, longer contract
- Confirm: Is it really price, or lack of confidence?

"We need to think about it"
- Uncover real concern: What specifically needs thinking?
- Timeline: When will you decide?
- Criteria: What will change your mind?
- Action: Schedule follow-up (specific date)

"We're happy with current solution"
- Curiosity: What if there was 10x better?
- Blind spot: What aren't they evaluating?
- Timing: When would you re-evaluate?
- Future: Will their needs change?

"Let me check with [colleague]"
- Normal, okay
- Ask: What will they care about?
- Help: Offer to join conversation
- Timeline: When will you have answer?

"Your competitor is better"
- Curious: What specifically?
- Honest: They may be right for them
- Differentiation: Where we're different
- Trial: Prove it in real use

## SALES PSYCHOLOGY

### Reciprocity
People want to return favors

Tactics:
- Give value first (content, help, demo)
- Genuine help without expectation
- Specific assistance solves problems
- Creates obligation to reciprocate

### Social Proof
People follow what others do

Tactics:
- Testimonials from similar customers
- Case studies with results
- User counts/logos
- Awards and recognition
- Social media followers/reviews

### Scarcity
People want what's hard to get

Tactics:
- Limited time offer (genuinely limited)
- Limited spots (genuinely limited)
- Exclusive access
- Early bird pricing
- NEVER fake scarcity (breaks trust)

### Authority
People trust experts

Tactics:
- Your expertise (credentials, speaking, articles)
- Third-party validation (awards, press)
- Industry recognition
- Testimonials from recognized people
- Data and research

### Liking
People buy from those they like

Tactics:
- Be genuine and authentic
- Find common ground
- Show genuine interest in them
- Build relationship, not just transaction
- Follow up even after sale

## BUILDING RELATIONSHIPS

### Long-term Customer Development

Stay connected:
- Regular check-ins
- Share relevant articles
- Invite to events
- Celebrate their wins
- Be available when they need

Expansion opportunities:
- Identify additional needs
- Cross-sell complementary products
- Upsell to higher tiers
- Introduce to other departments

Customer advocacy:
- Ask for referrals
- Feature in case study
- Invite to speak at events
- Get testimonials
- Include in advisory board

## SALES METRICS

### Activity Metrics
- Calls made: Effort measure
- Emails sent: Outreach volume
- Meetings held: Conversion of outreach
- Proposals sent: Qualified opportunities

### Conversion Metrics
- Conversion rate: % moving to next stage
- Sales cycle length: Days to close
- Win rate: % of proposals that close
- Average deal size: Revenue per deal

### Revenue Metrics
- ARR: Annual recurring revenue
- MRR: Monthly recurring revenue
- Pipeline: Total in-flight opportunity
- Quota attainment: % of target achieved

### Pipeline Management
- Top of funnel: Prospecting activity
- Middle: Qualification and discovery
- Bottom: Proposals and negotiation
- Total pipeline: Should be 3-5x annual target

## DIFFERENT SALES MODELS

### Inside Sales (Remote)
- Phone, email, video calls
- Efficient for smaller deals
- Quick cycle times
- Scalable
- Example: SaaS sales

### Field Sales
- In-person meetings
- For larger deals
- Longer cycle times
- High cost per rep
- Example: Enterprise software

### Sales Development Representatives (SDRs)
- Specialize in prospecting
- Qualify leads
- Set meetings for Account Executives
- Higher volume, faster cycle
- Example: Tech startup go-to-market

### Account Management
- Focus on existing customers
- Expansion and retention
- Support and advocacy
- Relationship building
- Example: Enterprise SaaS

---

## COMMON SALES MISTAKES

1. Talking more than listening: Miss real needs
2. Pitch too early: No discovery first
3. Wrong personas: Wasting time
4. No follow-up: Leads go cold
5. Desperate vibe: Drives people away
6. Overselling: Promises you can't keep
7. No qualification: Pursuing bad fits
8. Ignoring objections: Not addressing concerns
9. Not tracking: Can't improve
10. Wrong compensation: Incentivizes wrong behavior
