## BUSINESS STRUCTURE & FORMATION

### Sole Proprietorship
Definition: Individual owns business

Pros:
- Simplest to form
- Complete control
- All profit yours
- Easy accounting

Cons:
- Personal liability (sued personally)
- Hard to raise capital
- Limited lifespan
- No separation personal/business

Use: Solo freelancers, small services

---

### Partnership
Definition: 2+ people own business together

General Partnership (GP):
- Partners liable for actions of other partners
- All partners liable for debts
- Equal ownership unless agreed

Limited Partnership (LP):
- General partner runs business (liable)
- Limited partners invest money (not liable)
- Limited partners can't control

Pros: Shared resources, expertise, capital
Cons: Shared liability, potential conflicts, joint and several liability

Use: Law firms, consulting partnerships

---

### Limited Liability Company (LLC)
Definition: Business structure with limited liability

Characteristics:
- Owners called members
- Limited liability (personal assets protected)
- Pass-through taxation (taxed at individual level)
- Flexible structure
- Multiple ownership possible

Pros:
- Liability protection
- Tax flexibility
- Easy to form
- Flexible management

Cons:
- Self-employment taxes
- State filing fees
- Limited lifespan (dies with members in some states)
- Less formal structure

Use: Small to medium businesses, startups

---

### Corporation (C-Corp)
Definition: Separate legal entity from owners

Characteristics:
- Shareholders own company
- Board of directors governs
- Double taxation (corporate + personal)
- Perpetual existence
- Formal requirements

Pros:
- Strong liability protection
- Easy to raise capital
- Perpetual existence
- Easier to sell

Cons:
- Double taxation
- Complex compliance
- More expensive to form
- Shareholder agreements needed

Use: VC-backed startups, larger companies, planning to exit

---

### S-Corporation
Definition: C-Corp taxed as partnership

Characteristics:
- Pass-through taxation (single level)
- Liability protection
- Limits on shareholders
- Formal requirements

Pros:
- Single taxation
- Lower tax burden
- Liability protection
- Good if profitable

Cons:
- Complex compliance
- More expensive
- Shareholder restrictions
- Not ideal if losses early years

Use: Profitable businesses wanting tax efficiency

## CONTRACTS & AGREEMENTS

### Employment Agreements
What they define:
- Job title and responsibilities
- Compensation (salary, bonus, equity)
- Benefits
- Confidentiality obligations
- Non-compete clause
- Termination terms

Important clauses:
- At-will employment: Can be fired anytime (US)
- Severance: Payment if terminated
- IP ownership: Who owns work created
- Non-compete: Can't work for competitor
- Confidentiality: Keep secrets

---

### Independent Contractor Agreements
For freelancers and consultants

Defines:
- Scope of work
- Payment terms
- Timeline
- IP ownership
- Confidentiality
- Termination

Critical distinction from employees:
- No employment relationship
- No benefits
- No taxes withheld
- More control over how work done
- IRS scrutinizes classification

---

### Service Agreements
Between company and customer

Key terms:
- Services described
- Timeline
- Payment terms
- Deliverables
- Acceptance criteria
- Intellectual property
- Liability limits
- Confidentiality
- Termination

---

### Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDA)
Protects confidential information

One-way: Company shares confidential info with person
- Company protects information
- Person agrees not to disclose

Mutual: Both parties share confidential info
- Both protect information
- Both agree not to disclose

Typical duration: 2-5 years

---

### IP Assignment Agreement
Who owns intellectual property

Critical for startups:
- All work created belongs to company
- Employee/contractor assigns rights
- Done before work starts
- Protects company ownership

Should cover:
- All inventions
- Patents
- Copyrights
- Trademarks
- Work products

---

### Equity Documents
For founders and employees

Documents:
- Cap table: Who owns what %
- Stock option plan: Grants to employees
- Vesting schedule: Earn equity over time
- Stockholders agreement: Founder rights

Typical vesting:
- 4-year vesting schedule
- 1-year cliff (earn nothing first year, then 25%)
- Monthly vest thereafter
- Accelerates on specific events

## INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

### Patents
Protect inventions

What patentable:
- Processes
- Machines
- Compositions of matter
- Software algorithms (sometimes)

Protection:
- 20 years from filing
- Must be novel and non-obvious
- Requires disclosure

Cost:
- $1,000-15,000 to file and prosecute
- Worth it for core technology
- But takes 2-3 years to grant

### Trademarks
Protect brand names and logos

Duration: 10 years, renewable indefinitely
Cost: $250-500 per mark
Protection: Unique brand identity, logo, slogan

---

### Copyrights
Protect creative works

Duration: Life of author + 70 years
Automatic upon creation (don't need to register)
Cost: Free (registration optional)

Covers:
- Software code
- Written content
- Music and videos
- Literary works
- Designs

---

### Trade Secrets
Protect confidential information

Duration: As long as secret
Examples:
- Recipes (Coca-Cola formula)
- Algorithms
- Customer lists
- Manufacturing processes
- Business strategies

Protection:
- Keep it secret
- Limit access
- Non-disclosure agreements
- Non-compete agreements

## COMPLIANCE & REGULATIONS

### Data Protection (GDPR, CCPA)
Regulations protecting personal data

GDPR (Europe):
- Right to privacy
- Right to access data
- Right to be forgotten
- Explicit consent required
- Data protection officer

CCPA (California):
- Right to know what data collected
- Right to delete personal info
- Right to opt-out of sale
- Disclosure requirements

Implications:
- Privacy policy required
- Data security required
- Consent mechanisms
- Data breach notification

### Employment Law
Regulations protecting employees

Key areas:
- Minimum wage
- Overtime pay
- Safe workplace
- Non-discrimination
- Family leave
- Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)

Varies by jurisdiction heavily

---

### Tax Compliance
Obligations for all businesses

Requirements:
- File annual tax returns
- Estimated quarterly payments
- Payroll tax withholding
- Sales tax collection/remittance
- Keep financial records
- Business licenses

Penalties:
- Late filing: Percentage of owed taxes
- Underreporting: Penalties and interest
- Fraud: Criminal charges

## LIABILITY & INSURANCE

### Business Insurance Types

General Liability:
- Covers accidents and injuries
- Covers property damage
- Third-party claims
- Essential for physical business

Professional Liability:
- Covers professional mistakes
- Errors and omissions
- Negligence claims
- Critical for consultants, advisors

Product Liability:
- Covers product-caused injuries
- Defective product claims
- Manufacturing liability

Cyber Liability:
- Data breach coverage
- Privacy liability
- Business interruption from cyber attacks
- Growing critical

---

### Liability Limits
Important decisions:
- How much coverage needed?
- Who pays in multi-party situation?
- Insurance caps liability
- Personal guarantee situations

## CONTRACTS INTERPRETATION

### Key Clauses to Understand

Indemnification:
- One party holds other harmless
- Pays legal costs and damages
- Shield from third-party claims

Limitation of Liability:
- Caps damages one side can claim
- "Consequential damages excluded"
- Important for risk management

Force Majeure:
- Neither party liable for unforeseeable events
- Acts of God, natural disasters, pandemics
- Excuses non-performance

Termination:
- How can contract end?
- Notice period required?
- Cure provision (time to fix)?
- Consequences of termination?

## COMMON LEGAL ISSUES FOR STARTUPS

### Founder Disputes
- Unclear equity split
- Vesting disagreements
- Role conflicts
- Exit disagreements

Prevent with:
- Written founder agreement
- Clear vesting schedules
- Defined roles
- Dispute resolution process

### Intellectual Property Issues
- Employee/contractor created IP
- Unclear IP ownership
- Patent infringement
- Trade secret theft

Prevent with:
- IP assignment agreements
- Employee handbook
- Confidentiality agreements
- IP audits

### Investor/Fundraising Issues
- Term sheet misunderstandings
- Valuation disputes
- Board seat conflicts
- Follow-on funding problems

Prevent with:
- Experienced lawyers review terms
- Understand dilution implications
- Clear governance documents
- Regular communication

### Customer/Vendor Issues
- Scope creep
- Non-payment
- Contract disputes
- Termination conflicts

Prevent with:
- Clear written agreements
- Detailed statements of work
- Payment terms specified
- Dispute resolution process

## WHEN TO GET A LAWYER

### Do It Yourself (Save Money):
- Forming simple LLC (online services ok)
- Basic non-disclosure agreements (templates)
- Simple service agreements (templates)
- Understanding legal concepts

### Get a Lawyer (Worth It):
- Founder agreements
- Venture capital negotiations
- Complex employment issues
- Patent/trademark filing
- Significant contracts (big customer, big vendor)
- Litigation/disputes
- M&A/acquisitions

### Lawyer Types
- General counsel: In-house lawyer
- Outside counsel: Law firm on retainer
- Transactional lawyers: Contracts, deals
- Litigators: Lawsuits, disputes
- Specialized: IP, employment, etc.

## KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. Business structure matters: Tax and liability implications
2. Get agreements in writing: Memories are faulty
3. Protect IP early: Hard to fix after company formed
4. Founder agreements critical: Prevent conflicts later
5. Understand key clauses: Know what you're agreeing to
6. Data protection compliance: Not optional, growing regulated
7. Employment law varies: Know your jurisdiction
8. Insurance essential: Protects company and personal assets
9. Have legal budget: Invest in prevention, not litigation
10. Get professional help: Lawyers save money in long run
